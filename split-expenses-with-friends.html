Split group expenses fairly with Shader7 FairShare

How to use Shader7 FairShare to split a trip, dinner or shared house bill: equal or weighted shares, exact-to-the-cent rounding, the fewest payments to settle up, and a PNG or PDF report to share.

Money is the fastest way to make a great trip awkward. One person books the accommodation, another pays for fuel, someone covers dinner, and at the end nobody is sure who owes whom. FairShare by Shader7 sorts it out in three steps: add the people, enter what each paid, and get the shortest list of payments that settles everything, exact to the cent.

It is free, needs no sign-up, and keeps your bill in your browser.

Step by step

1. Add people

Type each person's name and press Add person. You need at least two. Not sure how it works? Press Load example to see a ready-made group.

2. Enter payments and share weights

For each person, enter:

  • Amount paid: the total that person spent for the group.
  • Share weight: how big a portion of the total they should carry. Leave it at 1 for an equal split.

3. Settle up

Give the bill a name (for example "Weekend trip"), choose the currency (RON by default, or INR, USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, CAD, SGD or AED), and press Calculate settlement.

Reading the results

FairShare shows:

  • Total spent, average paid and the number of payments needed.
  • Payment plan: who pays whom, the list of transfers that settles the group. If everyone is already even, it says so.
  • Breakdown: each person's share, with what they paid, their share and their balance (positive means they should be paid back, negative means they owe).
  • A live report preview.

Then share it with Copy summary, Download PNG or Download PDF. Start a new bill clears the form.

Equal or weighted shares

Weights let the split reflect real life:

  • A couple sharing one room can each stay at 1, or be entered as one person with weight 2.
  • A child might have weight 0.5.
  • Someone who joined for half the trip might have weight 0.5.

Each person's share = total × (their weight ÷ sum of all weights). With weights 2, 1 and 1, the shares are 50%, 25% and 25%.

How FairShare keeps it exact

Splitting money rarely divides evenly. FairShare works in cents and assigns any leftover cents once, in a fixed order, so the individual shares always add up to exactly the total. For example, 100.00 split three ways becomes 33.34, 33.33 and 33.33, not three times 33.33 with a missing cent.

How it finds the fewest payments

Once every balance is known, FairShare lines up the people who owe from largest to smallest, and the people who are owed from largest to smallest. It settles as much as possible between the first person in each list, moves on to the next person once someone is fully settled, and repeats. This greedy matching keeps the number of transfers low, usually one less than the number of people with a non-zero balance, or fewer.

Worked example (300 RON):

Person Paid Weight Share Balance
Ana 200 1 60 +140
Ben 100 1 60 +40
Cara 0 1 60 −60
Dan 0 2 120 −120

Payment plan:

  1. Dan pays Ana 120.
  2. Cara pays Ana 20.
  3. Cara pays Ben 40.

Three transfers settle everyone, instead of a tangle of everyone paying everyone.

Tips for stress-free group money

  • Agree on the split before the trip. Decide whether costs are equal or weighted, and whether personal extras (drinks, souvenirs) are shared.
  • Log as you go. Note who paid what each day in a shared note, then enter totals in FairShare at the end.
  • Use one currency per bill. If you paid in two currencies, convert to one first, or create two bills.
  • Share the report. A PDF or PNG of the payment plan avoids "I thought it was less" conversations.
  • Settle quickly. Ask everyone to pay within a few days while the trip is fresh.

Shared homes and recurring bills

For flatmates, run a new FairShare bill each month for rent extras, utilities and groceries. Use weights for a bigger room or a partner who stays most nights. Combine it with the monthly budgeting guide to plan your own share in advance.

Privacy

FairShare runs in your browser. The current bill is saved in local browser storage so you can come back to it, and nothing is uploaded.

Common situations and how to set them up

Situation How to enter it
Couple sharing one room on a trip Two people at weight 1 each, or one entry "Couple" at weight 2
Someone joined for 2 of 4 days Weight 0.5 for that person
Kids on a family trip Weight 0.5 (or whatever the families agree) per child
One person did not drink at a dinner Split drinks as a separate bill with only the drinkers
Flatmate with a bigger room Weight 1.25–1.5 for rent-related costs

Separate bills for separate decisions

FairShare splits one total across the people in that bill. When only some people shared a cost, make a separate bill for it. For example, on a ski trip you might have:

  1. Accommodation and food for everyone.
  2. Ski hire for only the people who skied.
  3. Car fuel for the people in each car.

Run each bill, then add up what each person owes across them. It takes a minute longer and avoids the most common source of arguments.

Settling up

  • Send the payment plan to the group as a PDF or PNG so everyone works from the same numbers.
  • Use the payment method most of the group already uses, such as UPI, bank transfer or a payment app.
  • Mark payments as done in your group chat to avoid double payments.

Frequently asked questions

How do weighted shares work?

Weights describe each person's portion of the total. With one person at weight 2 and two people at weight 1, the shares are 50%, 25% and 25%.

How does FairShare handle rounding?

It calculates in cents and assigns any remainder cents in a fixed order, so the shares always add up to the total.

Does FairShare store my bill online?

No. It saves the current bill only in your browser's local storage so you can return to it.

Which currencies can I use?

RON is the default, and you can choose INR, USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, CAD, SGD or AED. The currency is used throughout the results and report.

Can I share the result with my group?

Yes. Copy the summary as text, or download the report as a PNG image or a PDF.
