Money

Build a monthly budget with Shader7's free money tools

A simple monthly budgeting method that uses the Shader7 Hourly Salary Calculator to pin down variable shift income, FairShare to handle shared costs and Receipt Maker to track freelance income.

Budgets fail for two common reasons: the income figure is a guess, and shared or irregular costs are forgotten. If you work shifts, split rent with flatmates or invoice clients, both problems are real. Shader7 has three free browser tools that fix the inputs to a budget:

  • Hourly Salary Calculator: your real monthly pay from the shifts you work.
  • FairShare: your exact share of group and household costs.
  • Receipt Maker: proper records of freelance or side income.

This guide shows a simple monthly routine that uses them, plus two budgeting methods to choose from.

Step 1: Know your real income

If your hours change from month to month, do not budget from your base salary.

  1. Open the Hourly Salary Calculator and select the month.
  2. Enter your base salary and fixed base hours (or press Auto), plus your weekend, night, C-shift and public holiday multipliers.
  3. Use Quick-fill to apply your usual roster (for example Weekdays 8h), then adjust individual days.
  4. Read the Total gross salary, and its INR estimate if you send money to India.
  5. Your log is saved automatically in the browser, so you can compare months later. Download the A4 report for your records.

This is gross pay. Subtract tax and deductions using your last payslip as a guide to get your take-home pay, the number you budget with.

Tip: for a cautious budget, plan with your lowest month of the last three and treat anything above it as a bonus for savings.

Step 2: List fixed costs

Fixed costs are the same every month:

  • Rent or mortgage
  • Utilities and internet (use a typical month)
  • Phone
  • Insurance
  • Loan or credit card minimum payments
  • Transport passes
  • Subscriptions

Step 3: Work out your share of shared costs

Flatmates, couples and families often share bills unevenly: one person pays rent, another pays utilities, someone does the big grocery shop. Once a month:

  1. Open FairShare, add everyone in the household and enter what each person paid.
  2. Use share weights for fair differences, such as a bigger bedroom (weight 1.5) or a partner who is away half the month (0.5).
  3. Press Calculate settlement to see each person's share and the payments needed.
  4. Put your share into your budget, not the amount you happened to pay.

Download the PDF report and keep it with your monthly records so everyone sees the same numbers.

Step 4: Track side income

If you do freelance work, tutoring or repairs, issue a receipt for every payment with Receipt Maker and Export CSV each month. Adding up your CSV gives your side income total, and you will have the records ready at tax time. Budget only the side income that has actually arrived.

Step 5: Pick a budgeting method

The 50/30/20 method

Split take-home pay into:

Share For Example on 5,000 take-home
50% Needs: housing, bills, groceries, transport, minimum debt payments 2,500
30% Wants: eating out, hobbies, travel, shopping 1,500
20% Savings and extra debt repayment 1,000

It is quick to set up and works well if your needs fit inside half your income. If rent alone takes 40%, adjust the split (for example 60/20/20) rather than giving up.

Zero-based budgeting

Give every unit of income a job until nothing is left unassigned:

  1. Start with take-home pay.
  2. Subtract fixed costs.
  3. Subtract your share of shared costs from FairShare.
  4. Set amounts for groceries, transport and other variable needs.
  5. Assign savings and debt repayment.
  6. Whatever remains goes to wants.

Zero-based takes more effort but works well with variable shift income, because you rebuild the plan each month from that month's real pay.

Step 6: Review at month end

At the end of each month:

  • Compare planned and actual spending in each category.
  • Download the month's report from the Hourly Salary Calculator and run a fresh FairShare bill.
  • Move any surplus straight to savings before the new month starts.
  • Adjust next month's plan: more for categories you always overspend, less where you never do.

Building a safety buffer

Shift workers' income can drop when rosters change, so a buffer matters more:

  • First goal: one month of essential costs in a separate savings account.
  • Next goal: three to six months.
  • Pay into it automatically on payday, before you spend.

Worked example: a shift worker sharing a flat

Monthly gross pay from the Hourly Salary Calculator: 5,485 RON. After tax and deductions (from the last payslip), take-home pay is about 3,200 RON.

Share of rent and utilities 1,050 From the monthly FairShare bill with two flatmates
Groceries 600
Transport 150 Monthly pass
Phone and internet 80
Insurance 70
Money sent home 500 Checked against the INR estimate
Emergency fund 300 Paid on payday

In a lower month, the first things to shrink are wants and money sent home, not the emergency fund.

Common budgeting mistakes

  • Budgeting from the best month. Plan from a typical or low month instead.
  • Forgetting yearly costs. Divide annual bills (insurance, subscriptions, registrations) by 12 and set that aside monthly.
  • Not counting shared costs correctly. Budget your fair share, not what you happened to pay this month.
  • No buffer for irregular costs. Add a small "miscellaneous" line for things you cannot predict.

Frequently asked questions

How do I budget if my hours change every month?

Log your shifts in the Hourly Salary Calculator to see each month's real pay, and plan from your lowest recent month. Anything extra goes to savings.

Should I budget with gross or net pay?

Net (take-home) pay. The Hourly Salary Calculator shows gross pay before tax, so subtract your usual deductions first.

How do flatmates split bills fairly?

Enter what each person paid in FairShare and use share weights for differences such as room size. It shows each person's share and the fewest payments to settle.

What is the easiest budgeting method to start with?

50/30/20 is the quickest. If your income varies a lot, zero-based budgeting gives you more control.

Are these tools free and private?

Yes. All three run in your browser, need no account, and keep your data on your device.
