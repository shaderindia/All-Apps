Prepare for behavioural interviews from your CV Banao resume

Turn the bullets on your CV Banao resume into STAR interview stories, prepare answers to the most common behavioural questions, and keep your resume and interview story consistent.

Behavioural questions such as "Tell me about a time when…" are where many interviews are won or lost. The good news: you have already done most of the preparation if your resume is solid. Every strong bullet on your resume is the headline of a story, and an interviewer will often pick one and ask, "Tell me more about that."

This guide shows how to turn the resume you built in CV Banao into a set of ready interview stories, and how to keep the two consistent.

Start from your resume bullets

CV Banao encourages bullets built from three parts: Action, Method and Outcome. That maps neatly onto the STAR method interviewers expect:

Resume bullet part STAR part What you say in the interview
(context of the role) Situation Where you were and what was going on
Action verb + goal Task What you needed to achieve
Method Action The specific steps you took
Outcome Result What changed, ideally with a number

Resume bullet: "Rewrote the onboarding guide and set up weekly check-ins, cutting new-hire ramp-up from six weeks to four."

Interview story:

  • Situation: New support hires were taking about six weeks to handle tickets alone, and the team was stretched.
  • Task: My manager asked me to shorten ramp-up without adding training hours.
  • Action: I interviewed the last four hires about where they got stuck, rewrote the guide around the 20 most common ticket types, and started a 15-minute weekly check-in.
  • Result: The next three hires were independent in about four weeks, and the team lead adopted the check-in for all new staff.

Keep each story to about two minutes. Spend most of the time on the Action, and use "I" for what you did.

Build a story bank

Open your resume in CV Banao and go through each role. For every bullet with a clear outcome, write a STAR story in a few lines. Aim for eight to ten stories that together cover these themes:

  • A success you are proud of.
  • A failure or mistake, and what you learned.
  • A conflict or disagreement with a colleague or manager.
  • Leading or influencing without formal authority.
  • A tight deadline or competing priorities.
  • Learning something new quickly.
  • Going beyond your role for a customer or teammate.
  • Making a decision with incomplete information.

Most stories can answer several questions, so ten good ones cover almost anything.

Keep your resume and story consistent

Interviewers often have your resume open while you talk. Inconsistencies are easy to spot.

  • Numbers must match. If your CV says "cut costs by 15%", do not say "about 30%" in the interview.
  • Titles and dates must match what you wrote. Check them before every interview.
  • Only claim what you did. If a result came from a team effort, say so and explain your part.
  • Update both together. When you improve a story, update the bullet in CV Banao too. Your resume is saved in your browser, so you can open it, edit the bullet and export a fresh PDF in minutes.

The week before the interview

  1. Read the job posting and highlight the skills it asks for. Match each one to a story.
  2. Tailor your resume in CV Banao: move the most relevant bullets to the top of each role and adjust wording to fit the posting honestly.
  3. Export the right PDF: Print / Save text PDF for online applications, Download A4 PDF for a printed copy to bring along.
  4. Practise out loud. Record yourself on your phone. Check length (about two minutes) and filler words.
  5. Prepare three questions for the interviewer about the team, the first 90 days and how success is measured.
  6. Plan the logistics: route or video link, what to wear, a printed resume and a pen.

On the day

  • Pause before answering. Taking a few seconds to pick the right story is better than rambling.
  • Answer the question asked. If they ask about a failure, do not tell a success story with a small hiccup.
  • End with the result and, where it fits, what you learned.
  • It is fine to ask for clarification: "Do you mean a conflict with a manager or a peer?"

After the interview

Write down the questions you were asked and how each answer went. Add any new stories to your bank, and send a short thank-you note within a day that mentions something specific from the conversation.

Answering "Tell me about yourself"

This is usually the first question, and your resume is the script. A simple structure that fits in about 90 seconds:

  1. Present: your current role and the main thing you do, taken from the top of your CV.
  2. Past: one or two achievements from earlier roles that relate to this job, in one sentence each.
  3. Future: why this role is the logical next step.

Example: "I'm a customer support lead at a software company, where I manage a team of five and our help centre. Before that I was an agent for three years, and I rewrote our onboarding guide, which cut new-hire ramp-up from six weeks to four. I'm looking for a role where I can work on support operations at a larger scale, which is why this position interests me."

Answering the failure question well

Interviewers ask about failure to see honesty and learning, not to catch you out.

  • Pick a real failure with real consequences, not a disguised strength.
  • Own your part without blaming others.
  • Spend most of the answer on what you changed afterwards, and show that the change worked.

Video interview checklist

  • Test your camera, microphone and connection 30 minutes before.
  • Face a window or a soft light, with a plain background behind you.
  • Put your resume and story notes beside the screen, not on it, so you keep looking at the camera.
  • Close notifications and other apps.

Frequently asked questions

How many STAR stories should I prepare?

Eight to ten strong stories cover most behavioural interviews, because each story can answer several different questions.

How long should an answer be?

About one and a half to two minutes. Keep the Situation and Task short and spend most of the time on your Actions and the Result.

What if I do not have a number for the result?

Describe a concrete change instead: a process the team adopted, positive feedback from a customer, or a problem that stopped happening.

Should I bring a printed resume?

Yes, a couple of copies. In CV Banao, Download A4 PDF gives a print-ready copy that looks exactly like the preview.

Does CV Banao store my resume online?

No. Your resume is saved in your browser on your device.
