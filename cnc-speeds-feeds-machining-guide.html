CNC speeds and feeds with the Shader7 Milling Setup Planner

How the Shader7 Milling Setup Planner turns cutting speed, chip load and pass geometry into the RPM and feed your machine can actually run, plus G-code, chip thinning, MRR, cycle time, power, torque and the warnings it gives.

Most speeds-and-feeds calculators stop at the textbook answer: RPM and feed. On a real machine that answer often cannot run. The spindle tops out, the tool holder has a speed limit, or the feed is capped. The Milling Setup Planner in the Shader7 CNC Machinist hub takes those limits into account and tells you what the machine will really do, then shows the cutting speed and chip load you will actually get.

The three panels

The planner is laid out in three steps.

01 / Setup: describe your cut

Workpiece material. Pick a material to load starting values, or keep Other / my own cutting data and type your own. The list covers unalloyed, low-alloy and high-alloy steel (ISO P), austenitic stainless (ISO M), grey cast iron (ISO K), long-chip aluminium (ISO N), nickel superalloy (ISO S), and titanium and copper/brass, which need supplier data. Press Apply material starting values to fill in the cutting data.

Tool and cutting data

  • Cutting speed Vc (m/min), from the insert or tool maker.
  • Feed per tooth fz (mm/tooth), your target chip load.
  • Cutter diameter D (mm).
  • Effective teeth zc, the number of cutting edges actually cutting.

Pass geometry

  • Axial depth ap (mm) and radial width ae (mm).
  • Cutting path length L (mm), the in-cut distance for one pass.
  • Identical passes, the number of times that pass repeats.

Machine limits

  • Machine maximum RPM.
  • Tool maximum RPM, the limit marked on the tool or assembly.
  • Maximum cutting feed (mm/min).

Optional power check

  • Specific cutting force kc (N/mm²) from your tool supplier.
  • Available spindle power (kW) at the operating RPM, not the motor's nameplate power.

02 / Result: machine-ready values

Output Meaning
Program spindle The RPM to program, after the machine and tool limits
Program cutting feed The feed to program, after the machine feed limit, in mm/min and in/min
G-code A ready line such as S3500 M03 / G01 F700.0, with Copy G-code

Feed at selected fz The feed your fz asks for at the programmed RPM
Actual cutting speed The Vc you get at the programmed RPM, in m/min and SFM
Actual feed per tooth The chip load you get at the programmed feed

Copy setup puts the results on your clipboard for a setup sheet, and Copy link gives you a link that reopens the planner with the same values. When the cut is below 50% radial engagement, Use compensated fz raises the feed per tooth so the real chip thickness matches the fz you entered.

03 / Check before running

A reminder list: workholding, tool overhang, entry and exit, coolant, tool grade, machine torque and a final program check. The calculations describe steady cutting only.

The formulas behind it

RPM   = 1000 × Vc ÷ (π × D)
Feed  = RPM × zc × fz
MRR   = ap × ae × Feed ÷ 1000          (cm³/min)
Time  = L × passes ÷ Feed              (excludes rapids and tool changes)
Power = ap × ae × Feed × kc ÷ 60,000,000   (kW)
Torque = Power × 60,000 ÷ (2π × RPM)        (N·m)
hex   = fz × √(1 − (1 − 2·ae/D)²)           (below 50% engagement)

Program values are rounded down and limited by the lowest of the machine and tool RPM limits and the machine's feed limit. The planner then works backwards to show the actual Vc and fz at those programmed values.

Worked example: a 12 mm, 4-flute carbide end mill in low-alloy steel at Vc 150 m/min and fz 0.05 mm/tooth.

  • Requested RPM = 1000 × 150 ÷ (π × 12) = 3,979 RPM.
  • On a machine limited to 3,500 RPM, the programmed spindle is 3,500 RPM, and the actual Vc drops to about 132 m/min.
  • Feed = 3,500 × 4 × 0.05 = 700 mm/min.
  • With ap = 12 mm and ae = 3 mm, MRR = 12 × 3 × 700 ÷ 1000 = 25.2 cm³/min.
  • At 25% radial engagement, the maximum chip thickness is only 0.05 × √(1 − 0.5²) ≈ 0.043 mm. Use compensated fz would raise fz to about 0.058 mm/tooth to bring hex back to 0.05 mm.
  • The G-code line reads S3500 M03 and G01 F700.0.

Material starting values

The material presets come from Sandvik Coromant's short CoroMill Dura solid-carbide end-mill chart. The planner uses them like this:

  • At 30–50% radial engagement it uses the chart's 50% case (ap = 1 × D).
  • Above 50% engagement it uses the 100% case (ap = 0.5 × D).
  • Below 30% engagement you should enter supplier data that accounts for chip thinning.
  • Between listed cutter diameters, fz is interpolated.

These are reference values for one tool family, not universal recommendations. Links to Sandvik's milling formulas and the cutting-data chart are at the bottom of the app.

Warnings and what to do about them

  • RPM capped by machine or tool. The cut will run at a lower Vc than planned. That is usually fine for carbide in steel; consider a smaller diameter or a different tool if you need the speed.
  • Feed capped by machine. The real chip load is lower than you asked for. Too thin a chip rubs instead of cutting, which heats the edge. Reduce the RPM to bring fz back up, or accept a slower cut.
  • Power exceeds available spindle power. Reduce ap, ae or feed.
  • Radial chip thinning. Below 50% of the diameter, the planner shows the real maximum chip thickness (hex), which is thinner than fz. Chips that are too thin rub and wear the edge. Press Use compensated fz, then check the result is within the tool maker's limits.
  • High spindle power use. Above 80% of the available power, the planner reminds you to leave margin for tool wear and entry spikes.
  • Axial depth exceeds the chart case. Confirm that the tool maker allows that depth.

Drilling and circle milling

For holes, use the Drill Feed & RPM Calculator, which adds feed per revolution, peck cycles and rigid tapping. For bores and bosses milled with circular interpolation, take the linear feed from this planner and correct it for the circle using the formula in the ID / OD guide.

Worked example: slotting versus side milling

The same 10 mm, 3-flute end mill behaves very differently depending on radial engagement.

Slotting in aluminium (full width, ae = 10 mm): with Vc = 300 m/min and fz = 0.04 mm/tooth, RPM = 1000 × 300 ÷ (π × 10) ≈ 9,549 and feed = 9,549 × 3 × 0.04 ≈ 1,145.8 mm/min. At ap = 5 mm, MRR = 5 × 10 × 1,145.8 ÷ 1000 ≈ 57.3 cm³/min. The planner shows 100% radial engagement over a 180° arc.

Side milling (ae = 2 mm, 20% engagement, ap = 15 mm): at the same fz of 0.04, the planner warns that the maximum chip thickness is only about 0.032 mm, because the cutter touches the work over a 53.1° arc. Press Use compensated fz and fz rises to 0.05 mm/tooth, which brings hex back to 0.04 mm. The feed rises to 1,432.3 mm/min and MRR = 15 × 2 × 1,432.3 ÷ 1000 ≈ 43 cm³/min, with a real chip as thick as in the slot but far less load on the tool.

Comparing both in the planner, especially the radial engagement and power figures, is a quick way to decide which strategy suits your machine.

Troubleshooting with the numbers

Symptom What to check in the planner Typical fix
Rubbing, shiny wear, heat Actual feed per tooth Raise fz or lower RPM until fz is back in the tool maker's range
Chipped edges Requested fz and radial engagement Lower fz; reduce ae; check runout
Chatter Radial engagement and ap Reduce engagement or overhang; change RPM slightly
Spindle load alarm Estimated cutting power Reduce ap, ae or feed
Slow cycle time MRR and in-cut time Increase engagement within tool limits, or use a larger cutter

Frequently asked questions

Why is the programmed RPM lower than the requested RPM?

Your cutting speed and diameter asked for more than the machine or tool allows. The planner caps the RPM at the lower limit, rounds it down and shows the actual cutting speed you will get.

What does "effective teeth" mean?

The number of cutting edges that actually cut. For most end mills it is the flute count. For some indexable cutters with differential pitch or wiper inserts, use the value the tool maker gives.

Is the cycle time accurate?

It covers in-cut time only: length × passes ÷ feed. Rapids, acceleration, tool changes and approach moves are not included.

Where do I find kc?

Tool makers publish specific cutting force for each material group, often as kc1 at a chip thickness of 1 mm. Use the value for your actual chip thickness if you have it.

Does the planner work in inches?

It is metric: mm, m/min, mm/min and cm³/min.
