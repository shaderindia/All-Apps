Passport Photo Maker can crop, straighten and swap the background of almost any photo, but it cannot add light that was never there. Good lighting is the single biggest difference between a home photo that looks official and one that gets rejected. It also helps the app's background removal: a face that is evenly lit and clearly separated from the wall gives cleaner edges around the hair and shoulders.

Here are three setups that work with things you already have, and how to check the result in the app.

What good passport lighting looks like

Most authorities ask for the same things, even if the wording varies:

  • Even light across the whole face. No bright side and dark side.
  • No shadows on the face or on the background behind you.
  • No glare on the skin or glasses, and no red-eye.
  • Natural skin tone, not orange from warm bulbs or blue from shade.

Your goal is soft, flat light coming from in front of you.

Setup 1: a window (the easiest)

  1. Face a window with indirect daylight. Avoid direct sun, which makes harsh shadows and makes you squint. An overcast day or a north-facing window is ideal.
  2. Stand about 1 m from the window, with the wall you are using as a background behind you.
  3. Step 0.5–1 m away from the wall, so your shadow falls on the floor rather than behind your head.
  4. Have someone take the photo from between you and the window, at your eye level.

If one side of your face is darker, hold a white sheet of card, a pillowcase or a large book with white pages just out of frame on that side to bounce light back.

Setup 2: two lamps

When daylight is not available, use two lamps with similar bulbs:

  1. Place one lamp in front and to the left, about 45° from your nose, and one in front and to the right, at the same angle and distance.
  2. Raise both to slightly above eye level, pointing down a little.
  3. Diffuse them: shine through a thin white curtain or tracing paper, or bounce them off a white wall. Bare bulbs give hard shadows and shiny skin.
  4. Turn off overhead ceiling lights, which create shadows under the eyes and nose.

Use bulbs of the same colour temperature (both "daylight" or both "warm white"). Mixed colours give patchy skin tones that are hard to correct.

Setup 3: phone and a ring light

A ring light at eye level, directly behind the phone, gives very even light. Keep it far enough away (about 1 m) and at a moderate brightness to avoid a shiny forehead and a visible ring in your eyes.

Background and distance

  • A plain, light wall works best. The app can replace the background, but a plain wall still gives cleaner edges than a busy room.
  • Distance from the wall matters more than the wall colour. Standing away from it avoids shadows and helps the background remover separate you from it.
  • Contrast helps. If you have very light hair, a slightly darker wall gives the remover a clearer edge, and you can switch the background to white in the app.

Camera position

  • Eye level, with the camera level, not tilted.
  • 1–1.5 m away, using the standard lens (1×), not the wide-angle lens. Close wide-angle shots distort faces.
  • Turn off beauty filters and portrait blur. Filters change your features; portrait blur can confuse the background edges.
  • Take several shots and pick the sharpest one with eyes fully open.

Checking the result in Passport Photo Maker

  1. Upload the photo in Step 1. It stays on your device.
  2. Zoom in on the face. Look for a dark side, shadows under the eyes and glare on the forehead or glasses.
  3. Press Remove background and inspect the edges around hair and ears. Uneven light often shows up here first as a halo or a missing strand.
  4. Choose the background colour your document requires.
  5. If the edges look rough, retake the photo with more light from the front rather than trying to fix it afterwards.

Common problems and fixes

Problem Cause Fix
One side of the face darker Light from one side only Add a reflector or a second lamp on the dark side
Shadow on the wall behind the head Standing too close to the wall Step forward, away from the wall
Shiny forehead or nose Bare, hard light too close Diffuse the light or move it back
Dark eye sockets Overhead light Turn off ceiling lights, light from the front
Orange or blue skin tone Warm bulbs or shade Use daylight bulbs or move to a window
Rough background edges Low contrast or blur More front light, sharper focus, plain wall

A five-minute test routine

Before you take the final photo, do a quick test:

  1. Take one photo with your current setup.
  2. Open it in Passport Photo Maker and zoom to 2× or 3× on the face.
  3. Compare the left and right cheeks. If one is clearly darker, move the light or add a reflector on that side.
  4. Look under the eyes and nose for shadows. If you see them, lower the light slightly or add light from below with a white card held at chest height.
  5. Press Remove background and check the edge around the hair. If it is rough, add light to the background wall or step further away from it.
  6. Repeat until the test photo looks even, then take several final shots.

Light colour and white balance

  • Daylight (about 5,000–6,500 K) gives the most natural skin tones and suits white backgrounds.
  • Warm bulbs (about 2,700–3,000 K) make skin look orange and white backgrounds look yellow.
  • Phone auto white balance usually copes with one light type, but struggles with a mix of daylight and warm bulbs. Turn off the lamps when using a window, or close the curtains when using lamps.

Photographing children and older people

  • Babies: lay them on a plain white sheet and photograph from above with soft window light. Keep the light even across the face.
  • Young children: sit them on a chair with the wall behind them and someone they trust behind the camera to hold their attention.
  • Older adults: soft, frontal light is kindest and makes it easier to see the eyes clearly, which authorities check carefully.

Frequently asked questions

Can the app fix bad lighting?

It can replace the background and crop the photo, but it does not relight your face. Retaking the photo with better light is the most reliable fix.

Which background colour should I choose?

Use the colour your document requires; many ask for white or off-white. After removing the background in the app, pick White or a custom colour.

Is flash a good idea?

Direct on-camera flash usually causes glare, shadows behind the head and red-eye. Soft window light or diffused lamps work better.

Can I wear glasses?

Many authorities no longer allow glasses in passport photos. If yours does, tilt them slightly down and keep lights at 45° to avoid reflections.

Is my photo uploaded when I check it in the app?

No. Passport Photo Maker processes the photo in your browser.
